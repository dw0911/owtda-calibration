Sliding windows extract local persistent-homology features, but shared observations can introduce dependence across derived features and invalidate the joint exchangeability that random permutation relies on. This paper evaluates the finite-sample calibration of a test coupling a fixed persistence-diagram vector representation with a maximum scan. A window-mean counterexample shows that i.i.d.\ raw observations do not ensure exchangeable window features. Under fixed-window-count and fixed-observation-length budgets, naive window-level permutation (M0) and a dependent multiplier bootstrap (M1) are compared over 28 null configurations, with mean--covariance features (MOMENTS) as a non-topological comparator. At the 0.05 nominal level, all 20 overlapping configurations are materially anti-conservative for both representations, reaching 0.9756 for the persistent-homology representation; M1 lowers rejection rates in all 56 matched cells but is materially conservative in all eight non-overlapping configurations, so calibration is not uniform. The three prespecified contrasts --- overlap--non-overlap M0 gaps and non-overlapping-end M1 conservatism under equal DGP weighting --- are estimated at 0.5354, 0.5330 and 0.0266. Under a moment-matched circle-to-figure-eight alternative, size-adjusted power is 1.00000 versus 0.06203; a mean-shift negative control reverses the ordering, consistent with the translation invariance of Euclidean persistent homology. Lower rejection rates therefore do not imply nominal calibration, and sensitivity and error control must be assessed separately.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords}: persistent homology; change-point detection; overlapping sliding windows; exchangeability; finite-sample calibration
